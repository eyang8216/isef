B2: Proteins - Structure, Function, and Enzymatic Catalysis

📚 Subject: Biology 🎓 Level: HL (includes SL foundations) 📖 Syllabus Code: B2

Learning Objectives

  • B2.1.1: Explain the generalised structure of amino acids and how they form the monomers of polypeptides
  • B2.1.2: Describe the condensation reactions that form peptide bonds between amino acids
  • B2.1.3: Explain how amino acid sequences determine the three-dimensional conformation of a protein
  • B2.2.1: Describe the four levels of protein structure: primary, secondary, tertiary, and quaternary
  • B2.2.2: Explain how hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges, and hydrophobic interactions stabilize protein structure
  • B2.2.3: Relate protein structure to function using specific examples
  • B2.2.4: Explain denaturation and its effects on protein structure and function
  • B2.2.5: Analyze the relationship between genes, polypeptides, and proteins
  • B2.3.1: Describe enzymes as biological catalysts that lower activation energy
  • B2.3.2: Explain enzyme specificity using the induced-fit model
  • B2.3.3: Interpret graphs of enzyme activity vs. temperature, pH, and substrate concentration
  • B2.3.4: Explain enzyme inhibition: competitive, non-competitive, and allosteric
  • B2.3.5 (HL): Analyze enzyme kinetics using Michaelis-Menten graphs and calculate reaction rates

Key Definitions

Amino Acid
The monomer unit of proteins, consisting of a central carbon atom bonded to an amino group (-NH₂), a carboxyl group (-COOH), a hydrogen atom, and a variable R group (side chain)
Peptide Bond
A covalent bond formed between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of another through a condensation reaction, with the elimination of water
Primary Structure
The linear sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain, determined by the gene encoding the protein
Secondary Structure
Regular, repeating structures within proteins stabilized by hydrogen bonds between backbone atoms, including α-helices and β-pleated sheets
Tertiary Structure
The overall three-dimensional shape of a single polypeptide chain, stabilized by interactions between R groups including hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges, and hydrophobic interactions
Quaternary Structure
The arrangement of multiple polypeptide chains (subunits) in a protein complex, held together by the same types of bonds as tertiary structure
Denaturation
The loss of a protein's three-dimensional structure due to disruption of bonds maintaining secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structure, typically caused by heat, pH changes, or chemical agents
Enzyme
A biological catalyst, typically a protein, that increases the rate of a chemical reaction by lowering the activation energy without being consumed in the process
Active Site
The specific region of an enzyme where substrate molecules bind and catalysis occurs, with a shape complementary to the substrate
Activation Energy (Ea)
The minimum energy required for reactants to undergo a chemical reaction; enzymes function by lowering this energy barrier
Induced-Fit Model
The current model of enzyme-substrate interaction where the enzyme's active site changes shape slightly upon substrate binding to achieve optimal catalytic configuration
Competitive Inhibition
A form of enzyme inhibition where the inhibitor competes with the substrate for binding to the active site due to structural similarity
Non-competitive Inhibition
A form of enzyme inhibition where the inhibitor binds to a site other than the active site (allosteric site), changing the enzyme's shape and reducing its activity
Vmax
The maximum rate of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction when all active sites are saturated with substrate
Km (Michaelis Constant)
The substrate concentration at which an enzyme operates at half its maximum velocity (Vmax/2); a measure of enzyme-substrate affinity
