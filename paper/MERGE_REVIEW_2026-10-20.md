## 1. Method

Both trees were compared file-by-file, then word-by-word.

- `diff` over every file in `paper_submission` except `build/`, then `md5` on all of `sections/*.tex`.
- `git diff --no-index --word-diff=porcelain --word-diff-regex='[^[:space:]]+'` on each differing file, so single-word and single-number substitutions surface as individual `+`/`-` tokens.
- A trailing-whitespace-insensitive pass on top of that, to catch punctuation-only edits (dashes, commas) that would otherwise hide behind whitespace noise.

This was necessary: a first sentence-level diff missed the space-charge **sentence deletion** in `09_limitations.tex` and the en-dash corruptions in `06_numerical_results.tex`. Both are recorded below.

### Edit 7 — `06_numerical_results.tex:28` — **ACCEPTED, with two typographic fixes**

The one substantive improvement in the new version. Your version is a number dump:

> Initialization sensitivity is tested using five angle--apex-radius starting pairs for Powell optimization on the `31x45` grid. The new version states the mechanism:

> Whereas Figure 6 evaluates one-dimensional angle sweeps at fixed cap radii, initialization and parameter-coupling sensitivity are tested separately via unconstrained two-parameter Powell optimization on the `31 x 45` grid using five starting pairs of half-angle and apex radius. Allowing the optimizer to jointly vary the apex radius shifts the converged half-angles downward to 40.20°–42.79° as the apex-radius parameter expands to 0.0704–0.0887 model length units (0.70––0.89 mm …), while projected RMS residuals remain nearly flat between 0.01257 and 0.01333 Pa. Checked against the solver, and it holds:

| claim | evidence |

| Powell, two free parameters `(half_angle_deg, apex_radius)` | `solver/solver/optimization.py:364` `minimize(objective, x0, method="Powell", bounds=scipy_bounds, options=options)`; `ConeShapeBounds` docstring: "Box bounds for the two free shape parameters (half_angle_deg, apex_radius)" |

- Also hardcodes `Figure 6`. It becomes `Figure~\ref{fig:angle-sensitivity}`, which is the sensitivity-landscape figure the contrast is against, so the number survives any future rebuild.

### Edit 11 — `09_limitations.tex:3` — REJECTED (sentence deleted)

Your version ends the paragraph:

> Its space-charge closures are phenomenological tools for studying Poisson shielding trends, not predictive emission models.

The new version deletes it.

Why rejected: the sentence is true and load-bearing. `solver/solver/space_charge.py` implements a Gaussian closure and a threshold-activated closure, iterated as an under-relaxed fixed point (`config.py` `SpaceChargeParams`: `relaxation=0.5`, `tolerance=1e-8`, `max_iterations=50`), and the module docstring calls them "Space-charge shielding closures". They are not used anywhere in the reported angle comparison. Deleting the sentence silently upgrades the model's apparent scope: a reader would no longer be told the Poisson machinery is phenomenological. This is exactly the class of edit that must not be merged.
