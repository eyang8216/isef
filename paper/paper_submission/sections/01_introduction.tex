\section{Introduction}

Electrospray is applied in many advanced systems, from biomolecular analysis to satellite propulsion. In a typical setup, a conductive liquid is fed to the tip of a capillary needle (nozzle) held at an applied voltage relative to a grounded extractor electrode. As the electric field deforms the liquid meniscus, outward electrostatic pull (Maxwell stress) competes with the inward pull of surface tension (capillary stress). When these two forces balance, the liquid deforms into a conical equilibrium known as a Taylor cone. This geometry has a classical theoretical half-angle of $\alpha_{\mathrm{T}} \approx \SI{49.29}{\degree}$ (commonly referenced as \SI{49.3}{\degree}) in the ideal, charge-free, conducting limit \cite{taylor1964disintegration}.

Because emitter geometry and applied voltage determine onset stability, systematic parameter sweeps are essential for system design. However, full electrospray dynamics involve moving free boundaries, fluid flow, charge transport, and downstream jet breakup. Simulating these compounding effects usually requires expensive and computationally heavy commercial multiphysics platforms. \rev{An accurate field solution is necessary but not sufficient: the angle can only be recovered if the chosen observable actually separates neighbouring candidate geometries. A shallow stress-residual objective assigns similar values to different cone angles, so the inferred angle becomes sensitive to numerical settings and initialization. Commercial platforms document least-squares parameter estimation and user-defined global objectives \cite{comsolglobalobjective,comsolparameterestimation}, but they leave the choice of diagnostic to the user.}

\rev{This project therefore separates two questions. The first is whether a lightweight, mathematically transparent, reduced-order solver can capture onset-level electrostatic--capillary observables on standard consumer hardware. The second is which angle diagnostic --- minimization of the amplitude-projected stress residual, or crossing of the flank-field power-law exponent --- reliably resolves the cone angle once that field solution is available. The comparison examines sensitivity to grid resolution, domain size, outer boundary condition, flank-sampling window, and initialization.}

\rev{The choice of geometric diagnostic matters wherever cone angle is used as a process signal. Experiments in modified coaxial electrospinning correlate Taylor-cone angle with fiber diameter \cite{zhou2019relationships}, and optical monitoring links process behavior to fiber quality \cite{lunni2020monitoring}. Both motivate angle-based feedback, and both inherit the reliability of the angle estimate.}

\rev{In electrospray propulsion, propellant intercepted by the extractor electrode limits operation \cite{lyne2021thruster}, and the ST7 flight report documents how propellant reaching the extractor forced the thrusters to operate in a degraded configuration \cite{anderson2018st7}. Such cases motivate emission monitoring in which liquid-cone angle, plume divergence, and intercepted current describe different aspects of the same system. A cone-angle estimate contributes to that assessment in proportion to the confidence attached to it.}

Instead of replacing full multiphysics software, we develop a fast, two-dimensional axisymmetric Python solver focused on the core electrostatic--capillary stress balance (Figure~\ref{fig:system-schematic}). The framework combines an immersed-boundary finite-difference solver with an automated image-processing pipeline; the extraction chain is exercised on synthetic silhouettes, and the same balance diagnostics are applied to published cone-jet photographs.

\paragraph{Novel Contributions.} This work makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \rev{We show that amplitude-projected stress residuals are weakly angle-sensitive observables for the tested scale-free equilibria: the objective is flat to within its discretization floor over roughly $\pm\SI{1}{\degree}$ around the minimum, while the position of that minimum shifts over several degrees as the grid resolution, cap radius and flank-sampling window are varied, so the residual magnitude alone does not pin down a unique cone geometry.}
\item \rev{We evaluate flank-field power-law crossing ($E_n^2 \propto \rho^{p}$) as an alternative observable. Under the analytical Taylor far-field condition the half-angle at which the exponent crosses the theoretical $p = -1$ threshold is monotonic in the candidate angle and grid-convergent, recovering \SIrange{49.14}{49.23}{\degree} across refinement levels against an ideal \SI{49.29}{\degree}, and it varies far less across the tested settings than the residual-minimizing angle. The advantage is conditional rather than universal: with a grounded outer boundary the exponent never reaches $-1$.}
\item \rev{We deliver an accessible sparse solver, running production grids in approximately \SI{0.46}{\second} on a consumer laptop, compared against an independent body-fitted finite-element reference, exercised on twelve synthetic silhouettes, and applied as a consistency check to published ethanol cone-jet photographs.}
\end{enumerate}

The project has three validation layers. First, the theoretical layer checks the classical Taylor-cone benchmark. Second, the numerical layer verifies the finite-difference electrostatic solver using manufactured solutions and grid refinement, and then compares the two angle diagnostics under variation of the numerical settings and initialization. Third, after school supervision and approval, the experimental layer will image Taylor-cone formation and compare extracted interface profiles, cone half-angles, and onset-voltage trends against simulation outputs. This paper documents the theory, solver architecture, and the numerical and published-image comparisons completed so far.
