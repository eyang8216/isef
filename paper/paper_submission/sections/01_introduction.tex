\section{Introduction}

Electrospray is applied in many advanced systems, from biomolecular analysis to satellite propulsion. In a typical setup, a conductive liquid is fed to the tip of a capillary needle (nozzle) held at an applied voltage relative to a grounded extractor electrode. As the electric field deforms the liquid meniscus, outward electrostatic pull (Maxwell stress) competes with the inward pull of surface tension (capillary stress). When these two forces balance, the liquid deforms into a conical equilibrium known as a Taylor cone. This geometry has a classical theoretical half-angle of $\alpha_{\mathrm{T}} \approx \SI{49.29}{\degree}$ (commonly referenced as \SI{49.3}{\degree}) in the ideal, charge-free, conducting limit \cite{taylor1964disintegration}.

Because emitter geometry and applied voltage determine onset stability, systematic parameter sweeps are essential for system design. However, full electrospray dynamics involve moving free boundaries, fluid flow, charge transport, and downstream jet breakup. Simulating these compounding effects usually requires expensive and computationally heavy commercial multiphysics platforms.

This project investigates to what extent a lightweight, mathematically transparent, reduced-order solver can capture onset-level Taylor-cone observables on standard consumer hardware.

Instead of replacing full multiphysics software, we develop a fast, two-dimensional axisymmetric Python solver focused on the core electrostatic--capillary stress balance. The framework combines an immersed-boundary finite-difference solver with an automated image-processing pipeline; the extraction chain is exercised on synthetic silhouettes, and the same balance diagnostics are applied to published cone-jet photographs.

\paragraph{Novelty.} This work makes three primary contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Conditioning analysis of stress residuals:} amplitude-projected stress residuals produce a nearly flat optimization landscape near equilibrium, with an angle--radius ambiguity yielding approximately $\pm\SI{1}{\degree}$ angle resolution.
\item \textbf{Flank power-law crossing diagnostic:} flank-field scaling ($E_n^2 \propto \rho^{p}$) is introduced as a robust alternative observable. The half-angle at which the scaling exponent crosses the theoretical $p = -1$ threshold is monotonic and grid-convergent, recovering \SIrange{49.13}{49.23}{\degree} across refinement levels (compared to the ideal \SI{49.29}{\degree}).
\item \textbf{Accessible solver and balance diagnosis:} we deliver an open-source solver capable of running production grids in approximately \SI{0.46}{\second} on a consumer laptop, compared against an independent body-fitted finite-element reference and validated on twelve synthetic test images and published ethanol cone-jet photographs.
\end{enumerate}

The project has three validation layers. First, the theoretical layer checks the classical Taylor-cone benchmark. Second, the numerical layer verifies the finite-difference electrostatic solver using manufactured solutions, grid-refinement and scaling tests, and internal consistency checks. Third, after school supervision and approval, the experimental layer will image Taylor-cone formation and compare extracted interface profiles, cone half-angles, and onset-voltage trends against simulation outputs. This paper documents the theory, solver architecture, and the numerical and published-image validation completed so far.
