\section{Literature Review}

Taylor established the foundational electrostatic analysis of conducting drops in electric fields \cite{taylor1964disintegration}. In the ideal electrostatic limit the exterior potential satisfies Laplace's equation and the conical conducting boundary imposes a Legendre-function condition; balancing Maxwell stress against capillary stress yields an equipotential cone with a \SI{49.3}{\degree} half-angle. This result is a benchmark for the present work rather than a contribution of it.

Subsequent studies and reviews show that practical electrospray operation extends well beyond this static limit. Fern\'andez de la Mora reviewed the fluid dynamics of Taylor cones and cone-jets, emphasizing that cone formation, emitted current, and downstream breakup are separate physical regimes \cite{fernandez2007fluid}. Melcher and Taylor formulated the broader electrohydrodynamic stress-balance concepts at fluid interfaces \cite{melcher1969electrohydrodynamics}, and Saville reviewed the Taylor--Melcher leaky-dielectric model, which accounts for finite liquid conductivity, surface charge, and tangential electric stresses \cite{saville1997electrohydrodynamics}. Fern\'andez de la Mora and Loscertales studied the current emitted by highly conducting Taylor cones \cite{fernandez1994current}, Gañán-Calvo connected Taylor-type electrostatic solutions to cone-jet scaling laws \cite{ganan1997conejet}, and Hartman and coauthors modeled cone-jet electrospray behavior with additional physical complexity \cite{hartman1999electrohydrodynamic}.

These studies define the scope of our work. A reduced-order onset model should accurately reproduce the classical Taylor limit in the charge-free conducting regime without overclaiming full electrohydrodynamic prediction. Accordingly, our solver omits fluid flow, surface-charge conservation, and droplet breakup, instead focusing on a transparent axisymmetric electrostatic--capillary formulation with effective Poisson space-charge closures and image-based stress diagnostics. The intended contribution is accessibility and transparency: a sparse Python implementation that can be inspected, tested, and run on consumer hardware for onset-level parameter studies.
