\section{Numerical Verification and Sensitivity}\label{sec:completed-verification}

The tests below address distinct evidence questions: regular-grid operator accuracy, sensitivity to curved-boundary representation, angle-diagnostic behavior, and the image-extraction software path. The available results should not be read as interchangeable validation of the full physical model.

\subsection{Regular manufactured Poisson problem}
The source-derived quartic test uses $\phi=r^4+z^4+r^2z^2$ and the axisymmetric source $18r^2+16z^2$. On grids $17\times19$, $33\times37$, $65\times73$, and $129\times145$, the recorded RMS errors were $1.82\times10^{-3}$, $4.45\times10^{-4}$, $1.10\times10^{-4}$, and $2.73\times10^{-5}$, with consecutive observed orders 2.035, 2.018, and 2.009. Error is the RMS difference between numerical and exact potentials at interior nodes; for halved mesh spacings, $q=\log(e_h/e_{h/2})/\log 2$. The finite-difference results support approximately second-order convergence for this regular manufactured problem. Figure~\ref{fig:regular-check} also retains a Q1 finite-element curve from an independent analysis. The FEM implementation, mesh, and figure-generation source are not present in this repository, so that comparison is not independently reproducible from the supplied project files; this evidence gap does not imply that the plotted values are known to be incorrect.
\revisionfigure{0.98}{01_regular_poisson_fem.png}{Regular-grid manufactured-solution convergence. Finite-difference errors are measured against the exact potential; the displayed Q1 FEM series is retained from an independent analysis whose source and mesh are not included in this repository. $h$ is the geometric mean of radial and axial mesh spacings in model length units.}{fig:regular-check}

\subsection{Curved-boundary refinement}
The effect of curved-boundary representation is examined for the same rounded cone, with $30^\circ$ half-angle and $0.5$~mm apex radius, on grids of $31\times45$, $45\times65$, $61\times89$, and $87\times129$. Potential is evaluated at the same 56 gas-side locations on each grid. The inter-level change is
\[
D_\ell=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{56}\bigl(\phi_\ell(\mathbf{x}_j)-\phi_{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x}_j)\bigr)^2\right]^{1/2}.
\]
For the immersed discretization, $D_\ell$ decreases from 10.334 to 4.264 and 2.549~V. The staircase values are 25.731, 7.107, and 11.267~V; the increase at the finest pair shows non-monotone inter-level variation. These values characterize refinement sensitivity for this setup, not a general convergence order for either curved-boundary method.
\revisionfigure{0.98}{02_curved_boundary_refinement.png}{Curved-boundary refinement. Potential changes are evaluated at fixed gas-side points; the staircase sequence increases at the finest pair, so the displayed apparent orders are descriptive only.}{fig:curved-check}

\subsection{Fixed-cone finite-element comparison}
Figure~\ref{fig:fixed-reference} retains an immersed finite-difference (FD) versus body-fitted, piecewise-linear P1 finite-element (FEM) comparison for a charge-free problem. The plotted setup uses a domain radius and axial extent of 12.5 and 10~mm, a rounded cone of half-angle $49.29^\circ$ with apex at $z=8.6$~mm and cap radius $50~\mu\mathrm{m}$, a 1000~V conductor, and a grounded outer boundary. The FD grids are $65\times97$, $129\times193$, and $257\times385$; the fields are compared at common gas-side locations and at 41 interface positions over $z=3$--6~mm.

The retained figure reports finest-level relative RMS differences of 0.024\% for potential, 0.226\% for vector field, 0.381\% for interface-normal field, and 0.817\% for Maxwell pressure. It also reports 1.071\% for projected voltage and 2.518\% for projected residual normalized by the RMS centered capillary pressure. The FEM code, mesh, and generating script are absent from this repository; accordingly these values are reported as an external analysis result, not as a comparison regenerated by the present project. Their absence limits traceability but is not evidence that the numerical values are wrong.
\revisionfigure{0.98}{03_fixed_cone_fd_fem.png}{Retained fixed-cone FD--FEM comparison from an independent analysis. (a) Gas-point differences. (b) Interface-normal field. (c) Maxwell pressure at 1000~V. (d) Amplitude-projected residual. FEM source code and mesh are unavailable in this repository.}{fig:fixed-reference}

\subsection{Angle and parameter sensitivity}
Candidate half-angles from $46^\circ$ to $52^\circ$ are assessed across mesh resolution, domain size, cap size, flank-sampling window, and outer-boundary condition. Under the analytical Taylor far-field condition, the residual-minimizing angle lies roughly between $47.5^\circ$ and $50^\circ$, while the fitted $p=-1$ crossing lies between $49.07^\circ$ and $49.25^\circ$. These crossing values measure consistency with the imposed Taylor scaling; they are not independent predictions of a physical cone angle. With a grounded outer boundary, the fitted exponent shifts and does not reach $-1$, so the crossing diagnostic fails to return an angle. The plotted sensitivity figure is a retained campaign product: eight of its ten parameter series are reproducible with the current solver; the 25--55\% and 35--65\% flank-window series and the 1.50$\times$ domain series are not reproducible with the current exposed settings. This is a provenance limitation, not evidence that those plotted series are numerically incorrect.
\revisionfigure{0.98}{04_sensitivity_landscapes.png}{Angle sensitivity across retained campaign settings. The $p=-1$ crossing is available for the imposed Taylor far-field cases but absent for the grounded-boundary case. Three plotted parameter series cannot be regenerated from the current solver settings; see text.}{fig:angle-sensitivity}

Initialization sensitivity is tested using five angle--apex-radius starting pairs for Powell optimization on the $31\times45$ grid. Final half-angles span $40.20^\circ$--$42.79^\circ$ and apex-radius parameters span 0.0704--0.0887 model length units (0.70--0.89~mm on the 10~mm model unit), while projected RMS residuals remain between 0.01257 and 0.01333~Pa. This weak objective variation across distinct endpoints indicates angle--radius ambiguity in the fitted parameterization.
\revisionfigure{0.98}{05_optimizer_multistart.png}{Multistart optimization. Starting and final parameter pairs are shown with their projected residuals; apex radii are plotted in millimetres, where one model length unit equals 10~mm.}{fig:multistart}

\subsection{Synthetic-image measurement test}\label{sec:synthetic-completed}
The extraction pipeline is tested on analytic cone silhouettes with spherical-cap apices, rendered at $0.02$~mm/pixel for a needle radius of $0.4$~mm. The test crosses half-angles of $45^\circ$, $49.29^\circ$, and $55^\circ$ with Gaussian noise standard deviations of 1.3, 4.0, 12.7, and 40.0 greyscale levels, labelled as nominal signal-to-noise ratios of 40, 30, 20, and 10~dB. Image scale and symmetry axis are prescribed by the renderer; the noise realization uses seed 20260922.

Over the two lowest noise levels, the largest angle error is $0.027^\circ$ and the largest profile nRMSE is 0.62\%; all cases at these levels meet the preset $1^\circ$ angle and 2\% profile limits. Across all twelve cases, angle error remains below the angle limit, but at the highest noise level the $55^\circ$ silhouette has 10.5\% profile nRMSE. The nominal SNR labels are rounded labels for prescribed noise levels, not exact SNR values.

These results qualify the numerical image-processing round trip, not measurement uncertainty on recorded images: the fitted profile and reference profile share the same analytic source, and the test does not include illumination variation, contact-line pinning, optical distortion, or recorded-frame resolution. Section~\ref{sec:experimental-procedure} applies the workflow separately to published photographs. Its source photographs are cited, but the reduced images, extracted contours, and analysis intermediates needed to regenerate its reported measurements are not included in this repository.
