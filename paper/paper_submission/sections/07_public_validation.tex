\section{Published-Photo Image and Stress Workflow}\label{sec:experimental-procedure}

This section is a separate secondary-data application, not a validation of the exponent-crossing diagnostic or a calibrated physical measurement. It analyzes previously published ethanol cone-jet photographs and reported angles from Yang et al.~\cite{yang2025ethanol}; no new laboratory data are presented. The cited source figures are available through that publication, but the reduced source images, extracted contours, metadata, and scripts that generated the following overlays and values are not included in this repository. The results are therefore retained for interpretation, not claimed to be reproducible from the supplied project files.

\subsection{Image geometry and extraction}
The source study reports a flow rate of $Q=0.10~\mu\mathrm{L/s}$, nozzle-to-plate spacing $H=2$~mm, and aperture diameter $D=2$~mm. The photographs are taken from its Figure~5; full cone angles are digitized from its Figure~6 and converted to half-angles using $\alpha=\theta/2$. The numerical representation uses the reported geometry together with prescribed outer-domain, exposed-needle, and tip-regularization settings.

An axisymmetric $(r,z)$ representation places the nozzle exit at $z=0$ and the grounded annular plate at $z=2$~mm. The aperture and plate outer radii are 1 and 40~mm, respectively (Figure~\ref{fig:public-geometry}). The liquid surface and nozzle are prescribed at the applied voltage.

Four photographs at 1.80, 1.95, 2.10, and 2.30~kV are analyzed with the source scale bar, $300/191=1.57068~\mu\mathrm{m/pixel}$. A nominal grayscale threshold of 110, paired left and right boundaries for centreline registration, and linear flank fits over 30--75\% of visible height yield the displayed half-angles. The virtual tip aligns profiles; visible height is measured from nozzle exit to observed tip.
\revisionfigure{0.82}{experimental_photos.png}{Published ethanol photographs with retained contour and axis overlays. Photograph source: Yang et al., Figure~5. The underlying image-processing intermediates are unavailable in this repository.}{fig:public-photos}

The retained extraction increases from $22.26^\circ$ to $41.64^\circ$ as voltage increases from 1.80 to 2.30~kV, while visible height decreases from 301.6 to $147.6~\mu$m (Table~\ref{tab:angle-comparison}). The registered profiles show this widening and shortening.

\subsection{Comparison with reported angles}
For the three pairs matched within 0.005~kV (1.80, 1.95, and 2.10~kV), the retained absolute half-angle differences are $1.970^\circ$, $1.520^\circ$, and $0.520^\circ$, yielding a mean absolute difference of $1.337^\circ$ and RMS difference of $1.468^\circ$. These numbers compare two image-derived series from the published study; they are not a calibrated error estimate for this pipeline.

\subsection{Extraction sensitivity and fixed-pressure diagnosis}
Repeating the retained extraction at thresholds of 90, 110, and 130 gives a maximum half-angle range of $0.204^\circ$ for the matched cases and $0.322^\circ$ at 2.30~kV. Nominal straight-flank fit distances are 1.082, 0.837, 0.848, and $0.579~\mu$m in ascending voltage order. Threshold sensitivity, fit distance, and discrepancy between published series are different quantities.
\revisionfigure{0.98}{extraction_sensitivity.png}{Retained extraction sensitivity: threshold-induced angle changes and nominal straight-flank fit distances.}{fig:public-extraction}

For a fixed-pressure stress diagnosis, the reported half-angle at each voltage is used as a geometric input. Over the central 25--75\% of cone height,
\[
R(z;V)=\gamma\kappa(z)-\frac{\epszero}{2}E_n(z;V)^2-\Delta p,
\qquad \overline R=\langle R\rangle_A.
\]
The pressure offset is calibrated at 1.7203~kV,
\[
\Delta p=\left\langle\gamma\kappa-\frac{\epszero}{2}E_n^2\right\rangle_{A,V=1.7203\,\mathrm{kV}}
\simeq6.458~\mathrm{Pa},
\]
and then held fixed. The retained mean residuals are approximately 0, $-44$, $-123$, $-201$, and $-278$~Pa across the five reported conditions. They show that this fixed-pressure static closure does not balance the selected observed-angle series at the higher voltages. The displayed variations in local spacing, exposed-needle length, and tip radius preserve the negative trend; they do not establish the cause of the mismatch or validate a dynamic physical model.
