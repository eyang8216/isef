\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

This project compares two ways of reading a cone half-angle out of a reduced-order axisymmetric electrostatic--capillary solution. \rev{The amplitude-projected stress residual is a weak discriminator: its minimum is flat to within the discretization floor and shifts over several degrees as the grid, cap radius and flank-sampling window change, so the residual value alone does not pin down a geometry. The flank-field power-law crossing behaves better under the analytical Taylor far-field condition, where the half-angle at which the exponent passes $p=-1$ stays within $49.07^\circ$--$49.25^\circ$ across the tested settings against an ideal $49.29^\circ$. That advantage is conditional rather than universal: with a grounded outer boundary the fitted exponent runs from roughly $-0.75$ to $-0.63$ and never reaches $-1$, so no crossing exists and only the residual diagnostic is available.}

The solver itself couples an axisymmetric electrostatic solution to a Young--Laplace--Maxwell interface residual, reconstructs the interface field on a rounded cone, and recovers the classical Taylor half-angle. Its discretization is verified against manufactured solutions, against a staircase representation of the same curved boundary, and against an independent body-fitted finite-element discretization. \rev{A synthetic-image round trip of the extraction pipeline meets its preset $1^\circ$ angle and 2\% profile acceptance limits over the two lowest noise levels. Because that round trip qualifies the chain against analytic silhouettes only and does not calibrate it against recorded images, the published-image comparison of Section~\ref{sec:experimental-procedure} is reported as a consistency check rather than as a measurement: it yields a mean absolute half-angle difference of $1.337^\circ$ between two published image-derived series and a systematic negative residual trend under a fixed-pressure closure. The study therefore motivates the planned experimental validation rather than replacing it.}

Future work includes improving sharp-interface boundary representation, extending the diagnostic comparison to boundaries and operating regimes where the exponent crossing is unavailable, coupling threshold space charge inside the shape-optimization loop, adding more physically constrained charge/current closures, extending toward leaky-dielectric effects where justified, and comparing against higher-fidelity simulations or published experimental datasets. The intended contribution is not to replace full multiphysics simulation, but to provide a transparent, reproducible, and computationally accessible comparison of angle observables for Taylor-cone onset studies.
