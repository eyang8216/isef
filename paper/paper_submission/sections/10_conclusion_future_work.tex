\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

Within the tested reduced model, the amplitude-projected stress residual does not uniquely identify a cone angle: distinct angle--radius combinations and numerical settings can give similarly small residuals. With the analytical Taylor potential imposed at the far boundary, the fitted flank exponent crosses $p=-1$ at $49.07^\circ$--$49.25^\circ$, close to the ideal $49.29^\circ$ Taylor half-angle. This is a consistency result for the imposed far-field condition, not an independent physical-angle prediction. Its scope is made explicit by the grounded-boundary case, where the exponent never reaches $-1$ and the crossing diagnostic returns no angle.

The reproducible numerical evidence supports approximately second-order behavior for the regular manufactured Poisson problem, characterizes curved-boundary refinement sensitivity, documents residual ambiguity under multistart optimization, and qualifies an analytic-silhouette extraction round trip. The retained FEM comparison and published-photo workflow add context but cannot be regenerated from the current repository because their required artifacts are absent. The latter is a separate secondary-data image and fixed-pressure stress analysis, not validation of exponent crossing or full physical behavior.

Priority future work is to release the FEM mesh and source, archive reduced published-image inputs and extracted contours with their processing script, and regenerate every sensitivity series from declared settings. The next computational step is to test independently specified boundary conditions and compare the static model with higher-fidelity numerical solutions. Further secondary-data analysis would require archived published-image inputs and documented uncertainty; no new physical testing is proposed.
