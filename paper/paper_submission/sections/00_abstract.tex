\rev{Analysis of Taylor-cone formation in electrospray systems requires a computational description of the coupling between electric stress and surface tension; however, the computational cost of detailed multiphysics models can limit systematic parameter studies. To address this issue, this paper presents a lightweight axisymmetric electrostatic--capillary framework for use on standard computers. The method combines sparse finite-difference electrostatics, an immersed representation of the liquid interface, and gas-side normal-field reconstruction to evaluate the Young--Laplace--Maxwell stress balance. Projected stress residuals and flank-field scaling provide complementary diagnostics of candidate cone geometries. Numerical verification covers four systematically refined manufactured-Poisson grids showing approximately second-order convergence, a curved-boundary refinement study, a multistart study of the shape optimizer, and a synthetic-image round trip of the extraction pipeline that meets its prescribed $1^\circ$ and 2\% criteria on analytic silhouettes. That round trip qualifies the extraction chain against analytic silhouettes only; it does not calibrate the chain against recorded images, so the published-image comparison of Section~\ref{sec:experimental-procedure} is reported as a consistency check rather than as a measurement. The framework is offered as a reduced, transparent diagnostic tool; full cone--jet dynamics, unique physical onset prediction, and new laboratory validation remain future work.}
