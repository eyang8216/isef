\rev{Analysis of Taylor-cone formation in electrospray systems requires a computational description of the coupling between electric stress and surface tension; however, the computational cost of detailed multiphysics models can limit systematic parameter studies. To address this issue, this paper presents a lightweight axisymmetric electrostatic--capillary framework for use on standard computers. The method combines sparse finite-difference electrostatics, an immersed representation of the liquid interface, and gas-side normal-field reconstruction to evaluate the Young--Laplace--Maxwell stress balance. Projected stress residuals and flank-field scaling provide complementary diagnostics of candidate cone geometries, and an image-processing pipeline is specified for extracting interface profiles and half-angles from meniscus silhouettes. Within this repository, the regular manufactured Poisson component is verified: four systematically refined grids show approximately second-order convergence. The wider verification campaign reported in Sections~\ref{sec:completed-verification} and \ref{sec:experimental-procedure} --- curved-boundary refinement, an independent finite-element comparison, parameter-sensitivity and optimizer studies, a synthetic-image qualification, and a comparison against published ethanol cone-jet photographs --- was executed outside this repository. Its scripts and raw data are not included, and an in-repository rerun of the image pipeline did not meet its acceptance criteria, so those results are reported as supplied evidence rather than as reproduced verification. The framework is offered as a reduced, transparent diagnostic tool; full cone--jet dynamics, unique physical onset prediction, and new laboratory validation remain future work.}
