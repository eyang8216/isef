\rev{Reliable identification of Taylor-cone angles depends not only on the accuracy of the electric-field solution but also on the ability of the observable to distinguish candidate geometries. This study compares stress-residual minimization and power-law exponent crossing in the flank electric field as two angle diagnostics within a reduced axisymmetric electrostatic--capillary model. An independently developed sparse Python solver combines finite differences, an immersed representation of the interface, and gas-side normal-field reconstruction. Manufactured-solution tests and grid refinement assess numerical accuracy. The amplitude-projected residual objective is relatively flat near its minimum, so distinct cone geometries can attain similar objective values; under the analytical Taylor far-field condition, the half-angle at which the flank-field scaling exponent crosses $p=-1$ varies far less across the tested numerical settings ($49.07^\circ$--$49.25^\circ$) than the residual-minimizing angle does. That advantage is conditional: with a grounded outer boundary the fitted exponent never reaches $p=-1$, so no crossing exists as a diagnostic there. A synthetic-image round trip of the extraction pipeline meets its preset $1^\circ$ angle and 2\% profile acceptance limits over the two lowest noise levels; it qualifies the chain against analytic silhouettes only and does not calibrate it against recorded images, so the published-image comparison is reported as a consistency check rather than as a measurement. Unreliable angle estimates can misdirect voltage feedback and operating-state assessment, affecting electrospinning quality control and electrospray thruster monitoring. The lightweight workflow provides a basis for selecting geometric diagnostics and can inform the design of user-defined objective functions in multiphysics platforms.}
