This project develops a reduced, open-source, two-dimensional axisymmetric framework for studying idealized Taylor-cone electrostatic--capillary balance on consumer hardware. The solver combines sparse finite-difference electrostatics, electric-field reconstruction, a single-valued graph/cone-family interface, and a Young--Laplace--Maxwell stress residual with an optional projected voltage amplitude. The computational scope is deliberately restricted: the model does not resolve transient fluid dynamics, jet breakup, general charge transport, or a complete experimentally closed onset-voltage problem. Verification of the regular manufactured Poisson component shows approximately second-order convergence under systematic mesh refinement, while local tests cover axis regularity, graph curvature, pressure projection, and boundary precedence. The results support a reproducible reduced diagnostic framework, not yet a fully validated electrospray predictor. A proposed finite-element software cross-check and a supervised physical experiment are described as future validation routes.
