\section{Research Question and Hypothesis}

\subsection{Research Question}

The central research question is:

\begin{quote}
To what extent can a lightweight two-dimensional axisymmetric finite-difference solver---coupling immersed-boundary Laplace/Poisson electrostatics, electrostatic--capillary stress diagnostics, and synthetic-image extraction---reproduce classical Taylor-cone benchmarks and diagnose stress balances in experimental cone-jet profiles on consumer hardware?
\end{quote}

\subsection{Hypothesis}

We hypothesize that an immersed-boundary axisymmetric solver will converge at second order, agree with independent body-fitted finite-element solutions within $<1\%$ for interface pressures, and recover the classical \SI{49.29}{\degree} Taylor angle via flank-field scaling. Furthermore, when paired with contour extraction validated on synthetic benchmarks (targeting $<1^\circ$ angle error), the static fixed-pressure stress balance will quantify where real flowing cone-jets depart from purely static electrostatic--capillary equilibrium as voltage increases.

\subsection{Scope}

This study focuses strictly on onset-level axisymmetric geometry, interface field reconstruction, and static normal-stress balance. Downstream jet breakup, transient fluid flow, and plume dynamics are excluded to isolate how much physical insight can be extracted from a rapid, reduced-order electrostatic model.
