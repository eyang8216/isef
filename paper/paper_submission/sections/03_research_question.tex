\section{Research Question and Hypothesis}

\rev{The central research question is how} amplitude-projected stress-residual minimization and flank-field power-law exponent crossing differ as numerical diagnostics of a candidate Taylor-cone half-angle, and how boundary conditions and numerical settings affect each diagnostic\rev{.}

The working hypothesis is that the residual objective is weakly angle-sensitive after pressure and amplitude are projected out, whereas a $p=-1$ crossing is more stable only in the idealized configuration with imposed Taylor far-field data. Numerical verification distinguishes discretization accuracy from the ability of either observable to identify an angle.

\rev{To examine this hypothesis,} the study \rev{uses} a static, axisymmetric electrostatic--capillary model and a restricted family of rounded cones. \rev{The numerical comparison focuses on angle identification within this family. The published-image section provides a complementary secondary-data workflow for extracting geometry and evaluating fixed-pressure stress balance.}
