# Draft email to Mr George

**Subject:** Revised safety-gated plan for Taylor-cone solver validation

Dear Mr George,

Thank you for your thoughtful and detailed reply. We understand why the proposed high-voltage and solvent setup cannot be approved based on a general description, and we appreciate your concerns about electrical energy, earthing, ethanol vapor, ignition sources, engineering documentation, and the amount of supervision required.

We would like to continue pursuing hands-on validation of the solver, but only if the school determines that an approved design can meet those requirements. We are not asking you to accept the current apparatus description as safe, and we will not purchase equipment, assemble the testbed, introduce ethanol, or energize any system before the required reviews and approvals are complete.

We will prepare a complete safety-review package addressing your questions:

1. **Electrical safety:** a full circuit and interlock schematic, including the exact fail-safe state sequence, emergency shutdown, current limiting, access control, manufacturer-specified residual-energy discharge, and a supervisor-approved safe-state verification procedure. We understand that breaking mains power alone is not evidence that a high-voltage supply is discharged, and we will not open or modify the supply.

2. **Earthing and bonding:** a physical layout showing all protective-earth and bonding connections. We will ask the school’s facilities staff or a qualified electrical professional to identify and approve the actual protective-earth connection in the intended laboratory. We will not improvise a connection to a grounding braid or treat a student measurement as certification.

3. **Chemical and fire safety:** we withdraw the assumption that a sealed acrylic box is automatically safe. The revised plan will include the liquid inventory, safety data sheets, ventilation and vapor-control requirements, spill and waste procedures, and a review of whether a flammable liquid can be used near the proposed electrical system at all. If the school cannot approve that combination, we will either use an approved lower-hazard alternative or rely on another validation route rather than proceeding unsafely.

4. **Schematics and supervision:** we will submit complete electrical, fluidic, grounding, ventilation, optical, and physical-layout schematics, along with a risk assessment, operating and emergency procedures, approval requirements, and a realistic estimate of approximately 13–17 hours of supervised laboratory time spread across design review, non-energized inspection, dry commissioning, pilot work, repeated baseline trials, and closeout. This schedule is only a starting estimate and will be revised around your availability and the school’s requirements.

The scientific purpose would remain narrow: compare the solver’s predicted near-onset cone angle, interface profile, and onset-voltage trend with calibrated measurements under one controlled geometry, repeated trials, and a preregistered analysis. We would freeze the simulation parameters before examining the final measurements and report disagreement or uncertainty rather than tuning the model after the fact.

We have also prepared a revised safety-gated experimental design document for review. Could you please let us know which parts of this approach are feasible under school policy, who must approve the electrical and ventilation aspects, and whether the required one-to-one supervision can realistically be scheduled? If the school cannot authorize the physical experiment, we will follow your recommendation and complete the project as a computational study using numerical verification and published empirical data.

Thank you again for taking the safety and feasibility issues seriously. We would be grateful for your guidance before taking any practical step.

Best regards,

Curtis, Ethan, and Elliott
